# Tradewinds — A Captain's Handbook

*A merchant's saga of the Caribbean Sea. Trade, sail, fight, grow a fleet — and never stop.*

---

## The Goal

Tradewinds is an endless sandbox. There is no final victory — only the fortune you build, the fleet you command, and the legend you leave in every tavern from Havana to Bridgetown. Your run ends only if every ship you own is sent to the bottom. Progress autosaves whenever you make port.

You begin at **Port Royal** as a Level 3 captain with 400 gold, a single Sloop called the *Meridian*, six crew, and an empty hold.

## The Core Loop

Buy goods where they're cheap, sail to where they're dear, and sell. Spend the profit on a stronger ship, then repeat with bigger margins and bigger risks. Along the way you'll fight pirates, navies, and sea monsters; sign freight contracts; hunt bounties; collect legendary souvenirs; and maybe lose a ship or two.

## Trading

Seven goods move through eight ports. Every port produces some goods cheaply and pays a premium for others — Port Royal's rum is cheap, but Tortuga pays half again the going rate for it. In the Market, **▾ green means below the usual rate (buy)** and **▴ red means above it (sell)**. Prices drift a little every visit, so no route is ever exactly the same twice.

A rough map of who makes what: Havana (tobacco, sugar), Nassau (rum), Port Royal (rum, sugar), San Juan (coffee), Bridgetown (cotton, sugar), Willemstad (silk), Cartagena (spices, coffee). Tortuga makes little and pays well for vice.

Your hold is shared across your whole fleet. Each crate takes one slot.

## Setting Sail

On the Set Sail chart, tap any port to plot a course, then choose your route:

**Trade Lanes** — slower, calmer waters, fewer encounters. **Smuggler's Run** — faster, dangerous, but enemies drop richer loot. Crew wages (1g per head per day) are paid when you weigh anchor, so a big crew on a long crossing is a real expense.

Each crossing plays out as a series of legs. On any leg you might meet an enemy, a friend, a squall — or nothing but fair winds.

## Combat

Battles are turn-based with a type triangle:

**Cannon beats Boarding · Boarding beats Ram · Ram beats Cannon**

Super-effective hits deal 1.5x; resisted hits deal about two-thirds. Every ship — yours and theirs — has a type: pirates favor Boarding, navies favor Cannon, monsters fight with Ram. Each move button shows its **exact damage range against the current enemy**, colored green ▲ when super effective and red ▼ when resisted.

Your four moves: **Broadside** (reliable Cannon damage), **Chain Shot** (light damage, 55% to Tangle), **Ram** (heavy, 35% to spring a Leak), and **Boarding Party** (Boarding damage that grows with your crew — the bigger your company, the harder it hits).

**Status effects:** *Ablaze* burns each turn, *Leak* takes on water each turn, *Tangled* may cost a turn and blocks all escape.

**Crew abilities**, once per battle each, unlock as your company grows: the **Carpenter** (6 crew) repairs 30% hull, the **Master Gunner** (12) doubles your next attack, and the **Bosun** (18) clears every ailment and patches 10%.

**Fleeing** depends on your sails, your ship's speed against theirs, and luck. A failed escape gives the enemy a free shot at your stern.

**Navy parley:** hostile navies offer a choice before battle — fight, run, or buy an alliance. An allied nation's patrols never attack again; instead they salute, mend your hull, and press gold on you when you meet at sea.

## Levels & Experience

Your captain's level lifts your whole fleet: **+5% attack and +6% hull per level**. The thin gold bar beneath your gold and day readout is your progress toward the next level, which costs your current level × 120 XP.

There are two roads to experience. **Battle:** every victory pays 18 XP per enemy level, plus 10. **The sea itself:** every crossing pays 6 XP per day sailed, with 25 more for a delivered contract — so a trading captain levels too, just more slowly than a fighting one. Enemy strength scales gently behind your level, so growth always keeps you ahead if you spend your gold as well as your time.

## The Gunnery Master & Your Battle Plan

The shipyard's Gunnery Master sells seven maneuvers beyond your starting four — but you may carry only **four into battle**. Set your loadout in the **Battle Plan**: tap a benched maneuver, then the slot to replace. Swaps are free and unlimited, but only in port — choose before you sail.

The armory: **Grapeshot** (350g, moderate Cannon damage, 35% Ablaze), **Hot Shot** (700g, light damage but 70% Ablaze — a fire-starter), **Double Broadside** (900g, massive Cannon damage, 20% chance to spring your own seams), **Full Tilt** (800g, massive Ram damage, 25% chance to foul your own rigging), **Cutthroat Rush** (1000g, Boarding damage that scales harder with crew than Boarding Party and caps higher), **Brace** (500g, defense — halve the next blow and patch 8% hull), and **Smoke Screen** (600g, defense — 60% chance the enemy fires blind next turn).

Loadouts create real strategy. A monster-hunting plan wants Cannon coverage since Ram-type beasts resist nothing Cannon throws; a contract-runner wants Smoke Screen and Chain Shot to escape clean; a full Galleon crew makes Cutthroat Rush the hardest hit in the game. The reckless moves (Double Broadside, Full Tilt) pair naturally with Saltward, which wards their self-inflicted ailments for a whole voyage.

## Your Fleet Is Your Lives

If your flagship's hull reaches zero, she sinks — her share of cargo goes down with her, and survivors scramble aboard your next ship *mid-battle* to carry on the fight. When the last ship sinks, the run is over. Buy ships at the shipyard (Brigantine → Frigate → Galleon) both for their stats and as insurance.

Each ship carries her own upgrades, five ranks each: **Armor Plating** (-7% damage taken per rank), **Gun Batteries** (+10% attack), **Cut Sails** (+8% escape). Watch your ship's portrait in the Shipyard — plating, gun ports, and canvas appear as you refit her.

## Earning Gold Without Trading

**Freight contracts (Jobs tab):** the Harbormaster's Board posts three contracts per port. You're paid the *full fee the moment you sign* — enough to cover wages even when the purse is empty. Deliver without taking battle damage or losing cargo and the client adds a **25% bonus** on arrival. Storm damage from riding out a squall doesn't void the bonus; jettisoned crates and battle hits do. The voyage screen shows your contract in gold while the bonus is intact, red when it's forfeit.

**Bounties:** a WANTED poster sometimes hangs on the board. Take the contract, sail for the named waters, and the pirate will find you on the first leg. They're tougher than their level suggests, but the reward stacks on top of normal loot. Flee or fail and the hunt stays open.

## In Port

**Tavern.** Hire hands, and then some. *Rumors over Rum* (20g) reveals a port where one good will fetch nearly double — the port is starred ✦ on your chart, good for 18 days. *Dice with the Bosun's Mate* is Ship, Captain & Crew, the real sailors' game: find a 6, 5, and 4 in three throws, and your last two dice are cargo; high cargo wins the pot at even money. *The Sea Witch's Hut* sells one-voyage blessings: **Witchwind** (70g, first escape cannot fail), **Saltward** (90g, immune to all ailments), **Plunderer's Eye** (120g, +50% battle gold).

**Reputation.** Every docking counts. At 3 visits a port knows you (10% off refits, repairs, and hiring there); at 6 you're favored (15% off). Loyalty to a home circuit pays.

## Souvenirs of the Deep

Ten legendary relics are scattered across the open sea. Roughly **one leg in ten**, wreckage on the swell yields one — each a permanent boon to your whole fleet:

| Relic | Boon |
|---|---|
| 🔱 Neptune's Trident | +8% attack |
| 🐚 Barnacle Charm | +10% max hull |
| 🗺️ Silk Chart of the Ancients | +8% escape chance |
| 👁️ Kraken's Eye | +15% damage to sea monsters |
| 🧭 Golden Sextant | Trade Lane crossings one day faster |
| 🪮 Mermaid's Comb | 5% hull mended at every port |
| 🧲 Lodestone Compass | +3 cargo hold |
| ⚡ Powder Horn of St. Elmo | +15% chance your shots inflict ailments |
| 💠 Emerald Idol | +10% gold plundered from battles |
| 🔔 Bell of the Deep | -5% damage taken |

Your collection lives in the Captain's Log, mystery slots and all. Filling the case is the longest voyage in the game.

## The Captain's Log

The Log tab is your memory and your ledger: net worth (purse, fleet, and cargo together), your service record (crossings, battles, ships lost, bounties, contract clean-rate, and your net fortunes at dice), your last ten voyages, and — most valuable of all — the **Price Memory**: the last rates you saw at every port, color-coded, with the day observed. Read it before you buy. It is the closest thing this sea has to a cheat sheet.

## Friends on the Water

Not everything on the horizon wants you dead. Dolphin pods guide you past every remaining danger. The Sea Turtle Elder trades a crate of goods for sunken treasure. Merchant convoys sell below market. Allied navies escort, mend, and pay.

## A Few Words of Hard-Won Advice

Sail the Lanes when you're under contract and the Run when you're hunting. Chain Shot an enemy before you flee — a Tangled ship can't chase. Boarding Party is weak with a skeleton crew and brutal with a full one. Buy the rumor *before* you buy the cargo. Never sail your whole fortune in one hold on a Smuggler's Run with a single ship to your name. And when the dice are cold, walk away from the Bosun's Mate — he can play all night.

*Fair winds, Captain.*
